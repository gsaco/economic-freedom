# Literature map: Economic Freedom (EFW) and macro outcomes

Scope: EFW (Fraser) and related economic freedom measures, component analyses, dynamic methods, and measurement concerns (growth misreporting). Sources compiled via OpenAlex/Crossref. For entries with no abstract in OpenAlex, summaries are based on title/metadata and flagged for verification.

## Core EFW-growth literature

### de Haan and Sturm (2000) - On the relationship between economic freedom and economic growth. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0176-2680(99)00065-8
- Question: Does economic freedom relate to economic growth?
- Data: Likely cross-country panel using EFW (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Growth regressions; specification comparisons (per title; verify).
- Key finding: Investigates robustness of the freedom-growth link (details to verify).
- Limitation: Abstract unavailable; endogeneity likely; specification sensitivity.
- What we can improve: Use quinquennial LPs with transparent spec ledger and component shocks.

### Carlsson and Lundstrom (2002) - Economic Freedom and Growth: Decomposing the Effects. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1019968525415
- Question: Which components of economic freedom drive growth?
- Data: Cross-country EFW components (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Component-level growth regressions (per title; verify).
- Key finding: Decomposes aggregate effects into components (details to verify).
- Limitation: Static growth regressions; potential endogeneity.
- What we can improve: Dynamic LPs with component shocks and multiple-testing control.

### Heckelman and Stroup (2000) - Which Economic Freedoms Contribute to Growth? DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-6435.00132
- Question: Which sub-dimensions matter for growth?
- Data: Cross-country EFW subcomponents (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Component-wise growth regressions (per title; verify).
- Key finding: Identifies growth-relevant components (details to verify).
- Limitation: Static specification; limited dynamics.
- What we can improve: Event-study + LP dynamics and shock decomposition across areas.

### Justesen (2008) - The effect of economic freedom on growth revisited: New evidence on causality from a panel of countries 1970-1999. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpoleco.2008.06.003
- Question: Is there causal evidence from panel data?
- Data: Panel of countries 1970-1999 (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Panel causality tests / dynamic panel (per title; verify).
- Key finding: Revisits causality claims (details to verify).

### Doucouliagos and Ulubasoglu (2005) - Economic freedom and economic growth: Does specification make a difference? DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpoleco.2005.06.003
- Question: How sensitive is the freedom-growth link to specification?
- Data: Cross-country growth regressions (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Alternative model specifications (per title; verify).

### de Haan and Siermann (1998) - Further evidence on the relationship between economic freedom and economic growth. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1005072108159
- Question: Does freedom predict growth?
- Data: Cross-country growth sample (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Growth regressions (per title; verify).
- Key finding: Provides additional evidence on the freedom-growth association.
- Limitation: Static framework; limited identification.
- What we can improve: Quinquennial dynamics and component shocks.

### Heckelman (2000) - Economic Freedom and Economic Growth: A Short-Run Causal Investigation. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/15140326.2000.12040546
- Question: Short-run causality between freedom and growth?
- Data: Annual Heritage Foundation freedom components and growth rates.
- Method: Granger-causality tests on summary and component indices.
- Key finding: Evidence of short-run causality patterns varies across components.
- Limitation: Granger causality is not structural; annual noise.
- What we can improve: 5-year LPs with FE and crisis controls.

### Doucouliagos (2005) - Publication Bias in the Economic Freedom and Economic Growth Literature. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0950-0804.2005.00252.x
- Question: Is the freedom-growth literature biased by selective reporting?
- Data: Meta-analytic compilation of published estimates.
- Method: Funnel plots, meta-significance testing, bootstrapped tests.
- Key finding: Evidence of publication bias alongside a positive underlying effect.
- Limitation: Meta-analysis inherits heterogeneity in designs.
- What we can improve: Pre-specified spec ledger with BH/FDR.

### Hall and Lawson (2013) - Economic Freedom of the World: An accounting of the literature. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/coep.12010
- Question: What do EFW-based empirical studies find overall?
- Data: Review of 402 EFW-citing articles; 198 empirical uses.
- Method: Literature accounting and classification of outcomes.
- Key finding: Majority of studies associate EFW with "good" outcomes; few find "bad" outcomes.
- Limitation: Narrative review; no causal synthesis.
- What we can improve: Transparent replication with dynamic identification and null reporting.

### Williamson and Mathers (2010) - Economic freedom, culture, and growth. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11127-010-9656-z
- Question: How do culture and freedom jointly relate to growth?
- Data: Cross-country panel with cultural measures (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Growth regressions with culture interactions (per title; verify).
- Key finding: Culture may mediate or interact with freedom-growth link.
- Limitation: Culture measurement and endogeneity concerns.
- What we can improve: Regime heterogeneity and mechanistic channels (sound money).

### Bergh and Karlsson (2009) - Government size and growth: Accounting for economic freedom and globalization. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11127-009-9484-1
- Question: How does government size relate to growth when freedom/globalization are controlled?

### Bengoa and Sanchez-Robles (2003) - Foreign direct investment, economic freedom and growth: new evidence from Latin America. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0176-2680(03)00011-9

### Saini, Baharumshah, and Law (2010) - Foreign direct investment, economic freedom and economic growth: International evidence. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econmod.2010.04.001

### Graafland and Lous (2017) - Economic Freedom, Income Inequality and Life Satisfaction in OECD Countries. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-017-9905-7
- Question: Does inequality mediate freedom's effect on well-being?
- Data: Panel of 21 OECD countries.

- Key finding: Fiscal freedom, trade freedom, and deregulation increase inequality; sound money decreases it; inequality reduces life satisfaction.
- Limitation: OECD-only sample; subjective well-being measurement.
- What we can improve: Extend to global sample and SWIID uncertainty.

## Subnational and regional evidence

### Compton, Giedeman, and Hoover (2011) - Panel evidence on economic freedom and growth in the United States. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpoleco.2011.01.001
- Question: Does economic freedom predict growth across U.S. states?
- Data: U.S. state panel (abstract not available in OpenAlex; verify).
- Method: Panel regressions with state-level freedom indices.
- Key finding: Provides subnational evidence on freedom-growth link.
- Limitation: U.S.-specific; may not generalize.
- What we can improve: Cross-country quinquennial panel with harmonized components.

### Kacprzyk (2015) - Economic freedom-growth nexus in European Union countries. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/13504851.2015.1083076
- Question: Which EFW components matter for EU growth?
- Data: Panel of 28 EU countries using EFW areas.
- Method: GMM estimation to address endogeneity.
- Key finding: Positive relationship for four of five components (property rights, monetary policy, trade, regulation); results sensitive to method.
- Limitation: EU-only sample; relies on GMM validity.
- What we can improve: Global quinquennial LPs and multiple-testing control.

## Methods for dynamic effects

### Jorda (2005) - Estimation and Inference of Impulse Responses by Local Projections. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828053828518
- Question: How to estimate impulse responses without a full VAR?
- Data: Method paper (no empirical dataset required).
- Method: Local projections at each horizon with regression-based inference.
- Key finding: LPs are robust to misspecification and flexible for nonlinearities.
- Limitation: Efficiency trade-offs vs VAR under correct specification.
- What we can improve: Apply LPs to EFW shocks with FE and clustering.

### Sun and Abraham (2020) - Estimating dynamic treatment effects in event studies with heterogeneous treatment effects. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.09.006
- Question: How to estimate event-study effects with treatment heterogeneity?
- Data: Method paper.
- Method: Interaction-weighted estimators for staggered adoption.
- Key finding: Standard TWFE event studies can be biased; proposed estimators correct.
- Limitation: Requires richer data structure and assumptions.
- What we can improve: Use stacked event studies for crisis onsets.

### Callaway and Sant'Anna (2020) - Difference-in-Differences with multiple time periods. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.12.001
- Question: How to implement DiD with staggered treatment timing?
- Data: Method paper.
- Method: Group-time average treatment effects with flexible aggregation.
- Key finding: Provides robust DiD estimators under heterogeneous effects.
- Limitation: Requires treatment timing variation and common trends.
- What we can improve: Apply to reform bundles if timing is sharp.

## Measurement and data quality

### Henderson, Storeygard, and Weil (2012) - Measuring Economic Growth from Outer Space. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.102.2.994
- Question: Can night lights improve GDP measurement?
- Data: Satellite lights + national accounts.
- Method: Statistical framework combining lights and official GDP.
- Key finding: Optimal growth estimates combine lights and GDP; differences can be large for poor data countries.
- Limitation: Lights saturation and sectoral coverage biases.
- What we can improve: Use lights-based diagnostics for autocracy samples.

### Magee and Doces (2014) - Reconsidering Regime Type and Growth: Lies, Dictatorships, and Statistics. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/isqu.12143
- Question: Do autocracies overstate growth data?
- Data: Cross-country growth data with regime indicators.
- Method: Compares reported growth with alternative benchmarks.
- Key finding: Authoritarian regimes overstate growth relative to democracies.
- Limitation: Indirect measurement; alternative benchmarks noisy.
- What we can improve: Regime-stratified robustness and WB vs PWT checks.

### Martinez (2017) - How Much Should We Trust the Dictator's GDP Estimates? DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3093296
- Question: Are GDP estimates from dictators biased?
- Data: Working paper; title suggests use of alternative proxies (likely night lights).
- Method: Likely compares reported GDP to proxy measures (verify).
- Key finding: Evidence of overstatement in autocracies (per title; verify).
- Limitation: Working paper; details to verify.
- What we can improve: Measurement-error simulations and regime splits.

## Data sources used in this repo

### Laeven and Valencia (2020) - Systemic Banking Crises Database II. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1057/s41308-020-00107-3
- Question: Provide updated global crisis chronologies.
- Data: Crisis years across countries (banking, currency, sovereign).
- Method: Data compilation and validation.
- Key finding: Updated crisis dataset for macro-finance research.
- Limitation: Event timing and classification uncertainty.
- What we can improve: Align crises to quinquennial bins and test sensitivity.

### Feenstra, Inklaar, and Timmer (2015) - The Next Generation of the Penn World Table. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20130954
- Question: Improve international real GDP comparisons and productivity measures.
- Data: PWT version 8 with output-side GDP, capital, and TFP.
- Method: New benchmarking and measurement methodology.
- Key finding: Adds output-side GDP, multiple benchmark years, and capital/productivity series.
- Limitation: Measurement error in national accounts persists.
- What we can improve: WB vs PWT consistency checks and regime stratification.

### Solt (2016) - The Standardized World Income Inequality Database. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/ssqu.12295
- Question: Provide globally comparable inequality estimates with broad coverage.
- Data: SWIID harmonized inequality series.
- Method: Multiple imputation and calibration to LIS.
- Key finding: SWIID expands country-year coverage while maintaining comparability.
- Limitation: Imputation uncertainty and model dependence.
- What we can improve: Use uncertainty measures and robustness checks.

### Coppedge et al. (2015) - Measuring high level democratic principles using the V-Dem data. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0192512115622046
- Question: Construct high-level democracy indices from V-Dem indicators.
- Data: V-Dem expert-coded indicators, 1900-2013.
- Method: Aggregation of multiple indicators into democracy indices.
- Key finding: Provides polyarchy, liberal, deliberative, egalitarian, and participatory indices.
- Limitation: Expert-coded measurement error and aggregation choices.
- What we can improve: Use multiple regime measures and sensitivity checks.

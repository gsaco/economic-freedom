\section*{0) Executive Pitch}
\textit{(Constraint: $\leq$ 1 page; tight pitch + most defensible takeaway.)}

\textbf{Core question.} Do quasi-random changes in the \emph{market orientation} of elected governments---induced by razor-thin national elections---shift a country's economic institutions (measured by $\EFW$) and, through any channel(s), alter macroeconomic performance over the subsequent 1--8 years?

\textbf{Design spine.} The empirical core is a cross-country regression discontinuity design (RD) on close elections. The running variable is an ideology-based victory margin $m_{\cid \eid}$ between the ``more-market'' and ``less-market'' top competitors in a national election; treatment is a more-market victory $D_{\cid \eid}=\1[m_{\cid \eid}\ge 0]$. We pair this RD with \textbf{local projections} (LP) to estimate dynamic responses of (i) $\EFW$ and its sub-areas, and (ii) macro outcomes, at horizons $h\in\{1,2,4,8\}$.

\textbf{Two-part structure (and discipline about what is and is not claimed).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Part 1 (politics $\rightarrow$ institutions).} Reduced-form RD: close more-market victory $\rightarrow$ subsequent changes in $\EFW$ (first-stage / mechanism evidence). This is the most defensible causal claim in the project, subject to standard RD assumptions.
\item \textbf{Part 2 (institutions $\rightarrow$ macro).} Baseline is again reduced-form RD/LP: close more-market victory $\rightarrow$ macro dynamics. We then \textbf{optionally} layer an $\IV$ interpretation: use the close-election shock to instrument for $\Delta \EFW$ and report local-Wald / $\IV$-LP estimates as \emph{interpretive}, explicitly conditional on exclusion and monotonicity assumptions that may fail in political settings.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why this is publishable (Q1 referee-proof posture).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Master dataset contribution:} a canonical, audited master linking \emph{all} political datasets in \texttt{Archive.zip} (CLEA, DPI, NED, NELDA, ParlGov, V-Party) into a single election--party--government spine with explicit crosswalks, merge diagnostics, and quality flags.
\item \textbf{Identification discipline:} clear separation of (i) reduced-form effects of close political shocks, (ii) first-stage evidence for institutional change, and (iii) optional $\IV$ interpretation with weak-instrument-robust inference and falsification.
\item \textbf{Modern dynamics:} LP is default for dynamic effects, enabling pre-specified tests of asymmetry (reforms vs reversals), state dependence (interaction with baseline regimes), and nonlinearities (thresholds/splines), while keeping the estimand explicit.
\end{itemize}

\section*{1) Research Questions and Estimands}

\subsection*{1.1 Notation and objects}
Countries are indexed by $\cid$, elections by $\eid$ within country, and the election year by $t(\cid,\eid)$ with election date $d(\cid,\eid)$.
Let $p$ index parties/candidates. Let $\EFW_{\cid,t}$ be the Economic Freedom of the World index in country $\cid$ and calendar year $t$ (overall and sub-areas).

Define a \textbf{market-orientation score} $I_{p,t}$ for each party (or candidate's party) at the time of the election, built from party-ideology datasets (Section~3.3). A contest between the top two competitors in election $(\cid,\eid)$ yields a ``more-market'' side (higher $I$) and ``less-market'' side (lower $I$).

\subsection*{1.2 Estimands (Part 1: close win $\rightarrow \Delta \EFW$)}
Let $m_{\cid \eid}$ denote the \emph{ideology-based victory margin} between the more-market and less-market top competitors at the decisive round. Define treatment
\[
D_{\cid \eid} \equiv \1[m_{\cid \eid}\ge \RDcut].
\]
For horizon $h\ge 0$, define the \textbf{cumulative institutional response}
\[
\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h) \equiv \EFW_{\cid,\, t(\cid,\eid)+h} - \EFW_{\cid,\, t(\cid,\eid)-1}.
\]
The main Part 1 estimand is the RD \textbf{local average treatment effect of a close more-market victory} on cumulative institutional change:
\[
\tau^{\EFW}(h) \equiv \lim_{m\downarrow 0}\E[\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h)\mid m_{\cid \eid}=m] - \lim_{m\uparrow 0}\E[\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h)\mid m_{\cid \eid}=m].
\]
We estimate $\tau^{\EFW}(h)$ for overall $\EFW$ and for pre-registered sub-areas.

\subsection*{1.3 Estimands (Part 2: close win $\rightarrow$ macro dynamics; optional $\IV$ interpretation)}
Let $y_{\cid,t}$ be a macro outcome (e.g., log real GDP per capita, inflation, investment share). Define horizon-specific outcome transforms (LP form)
\[
Y_{\cid \eid}(h) \equiv y_{\cid,\, t(\cid,\eid)+h} - y_{\cid,\, t(\cid,\eid)-1}.
\]
\textbf{Reduced-form (causal under RD):}
\[
\tau^{y}(h) \equiv \lim_{m\downarrow 0}\E[Y_{\cid \eid}(h)\mid m_{\cid \eid}=m] - \lim_{m\uparrow 0}\E[Y_{\cid \eid}(h)\mid m_{\cid \eid}=m].
\]
\textbf{Optional $\IV$ interpretation (local Wald).} For any horizon $h$ where the first stage is nontrivial, define the local Wald ratio
\[
\beta^{\IV}(h) \equiv \frac{\tau^{y}(h)}{\tau^{\EFW}(h)}.
\]
We report $\beta^{\IV}(h)$ only as an \emph{interpretive} estimand, explicitly conditional on an exclusion restriction (close-win affects $y$ only through $\EFW$) and monotonicity. Because political shocks plausibly move many policies besides $\EFW$, this $\IV$ layer is not the baseline claim.

\section*{2) Identification Strategy --- Part 1}

\subsection*{2.1 RD identifying assumptions and why they are plausible here}
The design uses close elections as quasi-random assignment. The key identifying condition is continuity of potential outcomes and potential institutional trajectories in the running variable $m_{\cid \eid}$ at the cutoff:
\[
\E[\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h)(0)\mid m] \text{ and } \E[\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h)(1)\mid m] \text{ continuous at } m=0.
\]
We focus on elections with high-quality competition using pre-specified quality flags from \texttt{NED} and \texttt{NELDA} (Section~3.3), reducing scope for manipulated or non-competitive contests.

\subsection*{2.2 Constructing the running variable (margin) and treatment}
We define $m_{\cid \eid}$ from official vote/seat shares of the top two competitors at the decisive round, after classifying them by market orientation $I_{p,t}$. Construction uses \textbf{all} political datasets in \texttt{Archive.zip}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{NED (core election results spine).} National parliamentary and presidential elections with party/candidate vote and seat shares, election dates, and flags for coups/inconsequential elections (\texttt{Archive.zip/ned/presidential\_elections\_v2.dta}, \texttt{Archive.zip/ned/parliamentary\_elections\_v2.dta}; codebook \texttt{Archive.zip/ned/codebook\_elections\_database\_v2.pdf}). Variables used include \texttt{vote\_share1\_*}, \texttt{vote\_share2\_*}, \texttt{seat\_share\_*}, \texttt{country\_cow}, and \texttt{flag\_*} fields.
\item \textbf{V-Party (primary economic ideology for parties).} Party economic left--right scale \texttt{v2pariglef} (0=far-left to 6=far-right) from \texttt{Archive.zip/vparty/CPD\_V-Party\_CSV\_v2/V-Dem-CPD-Party-V2.csv}, with documentation in \texttt{.../V-Dem\_V-Party\_Codebook\_V2.pdf} (section ``Economic left-right scale (v2pariglef)''). We interpret higher values as more-market orientation (codebook clarification explicitly references privatization/lower taxes/less regulation on the right).
\item \textbf{ParlGov (government formation + alternative ideology scaling).} Coalition/cabinet formation and party positions from \texttt{Archive.zip/parlgov/parlgov.zip}: \texttt{cabinet.csv}, \texttt{cabinet\_party.csv} (PM party indicator \texttt{pm}), \texttt{election.csv}, \texttt{election\_result.csv} (vote share \texttt{vote\_share}, seats \texttt{seats}), and computed party-position view \texttt{viewcalc\_party\_position.csv} (e.g., \texttt{state\_market}, \texttt{left\_right}). Used for (i) validating which party governs after parliamentary elections, (ii) coalition-weighted ideology, and (iii) cross-checking ideology classification.
\item \textbf{DPI (institutional baselines and coarse ideology).} Country-year political institutions and coarse right/left coding of executives/government (\texttt{Archive.zip/dpi/dpi2012.xls}), including \texttt{system}, \texttt{execme}, \texttt{checks}, \texttt{checks\_lax}, \texttt{military}, and ideology fields such as \texttt{execrlc}, \texttt{gov1rlc}, \texttt{opp1rlc}. Used as predetermined state variables and a robustness ideology proxy.
\item \textbf{NELDA (election quality/competition flags).} Election-level indicators for whether opposition was allowed (\texttt{nelda3}), multiple parties legal (\texttt{nelda4}), choice of candidates (\texttt{nelda5}), and other irregularity/repression measures in \texttt{Archive.zip/nelda/nelda\_id\_q\_wide.tab} with definitions in \texttt{Archive.zip/nelda/NELDA\_codebook\_2011.pdf}. Used to define a pre-specified ``competitive election'' sample and quality flags.
\item \textbf{CLEA (validation and alternative margin construction).} Constituency-level election results with party vote shares and seats (\texttt{Archive.zip/clea/clea\_lc\_20251015.sav}; key fields documented in \texttt{Archive.zip/clea/clea\_manifest.json}). Used to (i) validate national aggregates (vote/seat totals), (ii) construct alternative close-election margins for parliamentary contests from aggregated party vote shares (\texttt{pv1}/\texttt{pvs1}) and seats (\texttt{seat}), and (iii) generate district-magnitude heterogeneity measures via \texttt{mag}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{2.3 ``Competitive close election'' sample definition (pre-specified)}
Baseline RD sample restrictions are applied \emph{before} looking at outcomes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Exclude} elections flagged as coups or inconsequential in NED (\texttt{flag\_coup==1} or \texttt{flag\_inconsequential==1}).
\item \textbf{Require} multiparty competition using NELDA (\texttt{nelda3==1}, \texttt{nelda4==1}, \texttt{nelda5==1}); if a NELDA match is unavailable for an election, keep but flag and treat as a robustness dimension.
\item Restrict to a symmetric bandwidth around $m=0$ chosen by standard RD selectors, with sensitivity analysis over a grid of bandwidth multipliers (Section~4).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{3.2 External (internet) datasets (only when additive)}
The ZIP contains political/election data but not $\EFW$ or macro outcomes. We therefore integrate \emph{four} high-quality external datasets, each chosen because it (i) materially improves measurement of institutions or outcomes, (ii) has transparent documentation, and (iii) is feasible to archive in a replication package (version + hash).

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Economic Freedom of the World (EFW) dataset (Fraser Institute).} Annual institutional outcomes. The EFW materials report coverage of \emph{up to 165 jurisdictions} and data extending \emph{as far back as 1970}, with the latest year reported as \emph{2023} in the 2025 annual report. Used as the institutional outcome in Part 1 and as the endogenous ``institutional shock'' in the optional $\IV$ layer.\\
\emph{Access:} free download (CSV/Excel) from \texttt{efotw}. \emph{Constraints:} citation required; archive the exact download.\\
\footnote{\url{https://efotw.org/economic-freedom/dataset} and \url{https://www.fraserinstitute.org/sites/default/files/economic-freedom-of-the-world-2025.pdf}.}
\item \textbf{World Development Indicators (WDI) (World Bank).} Primary macro outcomes $y_{\cid,t}$ (GDP per capita levels/growth, inflation, investment, trade openness, etc.). The WDI catalog reports temporal coverage \emph{1960--2024}.\\
\emph{Access:} World Bank API / bulk download. \emph{License:} CC BY 4.0. \emph{Limitation:} revisions and definition changes; we store vintage and cross-check with PWT.\\
\footnote{\url{https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0037712/world-development-indicators}.}
\item \textbf{Penn World Table (PWT) v10.01 (University of Groningen / GGDC).} Robustness macro series with explicit vintage files (PPP-adjusted real GDP per capita, productivity, capital stock, human capital). The Dataverse entry reports coverage \emph{1950--2019} and license \emph{CC BY 4.0}.\\
\emph{Access:} Dataverse DOI download; easy to pin version. \emph{Limitation:} model-based constructs; used as robustness not sole source.\\
\footnote{\url{https://dataverse.nl/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.34894/QT5BCC}.}
\item \textbf{Systemic Banking Crises Database (Laeven--Valencia, IMF).} Crisis/tail indicators (banking/currency/sovereign debt crises) to study whether institutional changes affect tail risk and to define baseline ``crisis state'' regimes. IMF publications describe coverage of systemic banking crises episodes during \emph{1970--2017} in the 2018 update.\\
\emph{Access:} IMF working paper page/PDF; archive downloaded files. \emph{Constraints:} follow IMF terms; cite the underlying paper.\\
\footnote{\url{https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2018/09/14/systemic-banking-crises-revisited-46232}.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{3.3 Master Dataset Construction (canonical schema + merge plan + audit checks)}
\textbf{Goal.} Build a single master dataset that can (i) support RD estimation at the election-event level, and (ii) support LP-style horizon regressions with consistent covariates and outcomes. The master has three linked layers: \textbf{(a) country}, \textbf{(b) party}, \textbf{(c) election-event}.

\subsubsection*{3.3.1 Canonical schema (tables and primary keys)}
\textbf{Country table} $\mc{C}$ (one row per country):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Primary key:} \texttt{country\_id} (internal), with canonical code \texttt{iso3}.
\item \textbf{Crosswalk fields:} \texttt{cow\_code} (from NED \texttt{country\_cow}), \texttt{ifs\_code} (from DPI \texttt{ifs}), \texttt{vdem\_country\_id} (from V-Party \texttt{country\_id}), \texttt{parlgov\_country\_id} (from ParlGov \texttt{country\_id}), \texttt{clea\_ctr} (from CLEA \texttt{ctr}), \texttt{nelda\_ccode} (from NELDA \texttt{ccode}), \texttt{ned\_country\_abb} (from NED \texttt{country\_abb}).
\item \textbf{Names:} harmonized \texttt{country\_name\_std} plus dataset-specific names to support audits.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Party table} $\mc{P}$ (one row per party ID, time-varying attributes stored in party-year table):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Primary key:} \texttt{party\_id} (internal).
\item \textbf{Crosswalk fields:} \texttt{v2paid} (V-Party), \texttt{parlgov\_party\_id}, \texttt{clea\_pty}, plus name variants: \texttt{party\_name\_std}, \texttt{party\_name\_raw\_vparty}, \texttt{party\_name\_raw\_parlgov}, \texttt{party\_name\_raw\_ned}, \texttt{party\_name\_raw\_clea}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Party-year table} $\mc{P}_t$ (party $\times$ year):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Keys:} (\texttt{party\_id}, \texttt{country\_id}, \texttt{year}).
\item \textbf{Ideology:} canonical \texttt{ideo\_econ} (primary from V-Party \texttt{v2pariglef}); secondary \texttt{ideo\_state\_market} and \texttt{ideo\_left\_right} (from ParlGov \texttt{viewcalc\_party\_position.csv}); tertiary coarse categories from DPI when party mapping is unavailable.
\item \textbf{Electoral strength:} vote/seat shares when available (V-Party party-year shares; ParlGov election results; CLEA aggregates), primarily for validation and as controls in some robustness checks.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Election-event table} $\mc{E}$ (one row per election contest used for RD/LP):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Primary key:} \texttt{election\_id} (internal), constructed from (\texttt{country\_id}, election date, \texttt{office\_type}, \texttt{round}).
\item \textbf{Core fields:} \texttt{office\_type} $\in\{$presidential, parliamentary$\}$; \texttt{date} (ISO); \texttt{year}; \texttt{round} (final decisive); \texttt{incumbent\_bloc} (when available).
\item \textbf{Top competitors:} standardized IDs \texttt{winner\_party\_id}, \texttt{runnerup\_party\_id}; plus raw names; leader/candidate names where available (NED presidential \texttt{candidate\_*}).
\item \textbf{RD objects:} running variable \texttt{margin\_market} $=m_{\cid \eid}$; treatment \texttt{D\_market\_win}; bandwidth-ready fields (absolute margin, bin indicators).
\item \textbf{Quality flags:} NED flags (\texttt{flag\_coup}, \texttt{flag\_inconsequential}, \texttt{flag\_unopposed}/\texttt{flag\_indirect}); NELDA competitiveness flags (\texttt{nelda3}, \texttt{nelda4}, \texttt{nelda5}) and selected irregularity indicators; CLEA/ParlGov validation flags (Section~3.3.4).
\item \textbf{Baseline states ($t-1$):} predetermined macro and institutional covariates $\bX_{\cid,t-1}$, including $\EFW_{\cid,t-1}$, GDP per capita, inflation regime, and DPI institutional measures.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{3.3.2 Crosswalk strategy (country codes, party IDs, election matching)}
\textbf{Country crosswalk (ISO3 as canonical).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Anchor on \textbf{NED} because it supplies both numeric COW code (\texttt{country\_cow}) and a stable country name (\texttt{country}) across the full election series. Map NED countries to ISO3 using a deterministic name match plus audited fuzzy matching against \texttt{external\_country\_iso.csv} in ParlGov (\texttt{Archive.zip/parlgov/parlgov.zip/external\_country\_iso.csv}). Ambiguous matches are resolved by a small manually curated override list stored with the project (audit-logged).
\item Propagate ISO3 to \textbf{NELDA} and \textbf{V-Party} via COW code links (NELDA \texttt{ccode}; V-Party \texttt{COWcode}).
\item Map \textbf{DPI} via a parallel name-based crosswalk (\texttt{countryname}) and retain \texttt{ifs} code as a secondary identifier for audit.
\item Map \textbf{CLEA} via \texttt{ctr\_n} name and retain \texttt{ctr} as secondary identifier.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Party ID crosswalk (internal party\_id).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Use \textbf{V-Party} (\texttt{v2paid}) as the primary anchor for party IDs where available (broad country coverage and explicit party coding units PDF in \texttt{Archive.zip/vparty/.../V-Dem\_Party\_Coding\_Units\_V2.pdf}).
\item For \textbf{ParlGov} countries, link ParlGov \texttt{party.csv} (\texttt{party\_id}, \texttt{name}, \texttt{name\_english}) to V-Party parties within the same country using standardized party names and year overlap; store match confidence scores and manual overrides.
\item For \textbf{NED} and \textbf{CLEA}, link raw party strings/codes (\texttt{party\_1}, \texttt{pty\_n}) to the internal \texttt{party\_id} using (country, election date/year, name) matching. When ambiguity persists, fall back to ``top-two only'' matching within each election (local matching) and flag low-confidence links for exclusion/robustness.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Election matching across datasets.}
Election-event table is built from NED as the backbone, then matched to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item ParlGov election/cabinet entries via (ISO3/country, date within tolerance window, parliamentary office type); used to confirm government formation (PM party) and coalition.
\item NELDA election records via (COW code, year, type, and election date where available), used solely for quality flags (not outcomes).
\item CLEA elections via (country, year, month; plus chamber type), used for validation and alternative margin computations.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{3.3.3 Canonical variables (what goes into the master)}
\textbf{Running variable and RD-ready margins.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \texttt{margin\_market} ($m_{\cid \eid}$): vote-share margin between more-market and less-market top competitors in the decisive round (presidential: final round vote shares; parliamentary: seat-share or aggregated vote-share margin depending on availability; both stored).
\item \texttt{margin\_abs} $=|m_{\cid \eid}|$, \texttt{bin\_margin} for McCrary-style density and heaping checks.
\item \texttt{bandwidth\_flag} fields for each pre-registered bandwidth rule (CCT/IK and multiples).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Political objects.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \texttt{winner\_party\_id}, \texttt{runnerup\_party\_id}; \texttt{winner\_leader\_name} when available (NED presidential \texttt{candidate\_*}; ParlGov PM when available).
\item \texttt{ideo\_winner} and \texttt{ideo\_runnerup}: election-time ideology scores, primarily from V-Party \texttt{v2pariglef}.
\item \texttt{govt\_formation\_validated}: indicator that the election winner actually forms government (parliamentary); validated via ParlGov cabinet PM party. Non-validated cases are retained but flagged for robustness.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{$\EFW$ objects.}
We ingest Fraser's annual $\EFW$ dataset and map its provided fields into canonical names:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \texttt{efw\_overall} ($\EFW_{\cid,t}$) and \texttt{efw\_area1}--\texttt{efw\_area5} (five areas), plus a pre-specified subset of component/subcomponent measures where consistently available.
\item \texttt{d\_efw\_1y} ($\EFW_{\cid,t}-\EFW_{\cid,t-1}$) and \texttt{d\_efw\_cum(h)} ($\EFW_{\cid,t+h}-\EFW_{\cid,t-1}$) for LP/RD horizons.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Macro outcomes.}
Primary outcomes come from WDI, with PWT as robustness:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Levels and growth: log real GDP per capita; GDP per capita growth; log price level / inflation (as available).
\item Investment and real activity: investment share, trade openness, unemployment (where coverage allows).
\item Tail/crisis outcomes: crisis incidence indicators from Laeven--Valencia; additional tail measures based on distributional cutoffs of growth/inflation (pre-specified).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{3.3.4 Reproducibility and audit layer (non-negotiable checks)}
The master dataset build produces machine-readable audit artifacts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Merge-rate tables} for each join (country crosswalk, party crosswalk, election matching), reporting matched/unmatched counts and shares by dataset and by year.
\item \textbf{Duplicate detection} for elections: within-country near-duplicate dates and repeated election types; explicit resolution rules stored in a log.
\item \textbf{Internal invariants:} (i) NED vote/seat shares monotone ordering of top competitors; (ii) CLEA constituency-party vote shares sum checks within election; (iii) ParlGov cabinet dates ordered and PM uniqueness; (iv) NELDA competition indicators consistent with NED flags where overlapping.
\item \textbf{Quality flags} propagated to estimation samples: non-competitive elections (NELDA), coups/inconsequential (NED), coalition anomalies (ParlGov), and low-confidence party matches (party crosswalk). Baseline RD uses only high-quality elections; all others enter as robustness strata.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{4) Empirical Specifications}

\subsection*{4.1 Baseline reduced-form RD (Part 1 and Part 2)}
For each horizon $h$ and outcome $Z_{\cid \eid}(h)$ (either $\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h)$ or $Y_{\cid \eid}(h)$), estimate:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\cid \eid}(h) = \alpha_h + \tau_h D_{\cid \eid} + g_{h}^{-}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}<0] + g_{h}^{+}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}\ge 0] + \bX_{\cid,\, t(\cid,\eid)-1}'\gamma_h + u_{\cid \eid h},
\label{eq:rd}
\end{equation}
where $g_h^{-},g_h^{+}$ are local polynomials of order 1 or 2 with robust bias correction, and $\bX_{\cid,t-1}$ is a pre-specified set of predetermined covariates (macro baselines, DPI institutional baselines, election-type fixed effects).

\textbf{Estimation and inference.} We use modern RD inference (robust bias-corrected CIs; multiple bandwidths; clustering at the country level when pooling across countries) as in \citet{CalonicoEtal2014,CalonicoEtal2019}. The default bandwidth is the MSE-optimal selector with sensitivity to $\{0.5,1,2\}\times$ that bandwidth.

\subsection*{4.2 First-stage validation (Part 1 as the mechanism core)}
Part 1 is primarily the first-stage validation:
\[
\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h) \text{ on } D_{\cid \eid} \text{ using (\ref{eq:rd}) for } h\in\{0,1,2,4,8\}.
\]
We require: (i) pre-trends in $\EFW$ absent in placebo horizons ($h<0$), and (ii) stronger effects in sub-areas plausibly tied to the ideology shift (pre-specified using party platform proxies), while acknowledging that $\EFW$ aggregates many policies.

\subsection*{4.3 OPTIONAL IV-RD / local Wald (interpretation layer only)}
Where the first stage is strong, we optionally estimate a local Wald ratio:
\[
\widehat{\beta}^{\IV}(h) = \frac{\widehat{\tau}^{y}(h)}{\widehat{\tau}^{\EFW}(h)}.
\]
\textbf{Discipline:} we present this only as an interpretation (not baseline identification) because close elections can change many policies besides those captured by $\EFW$ (violating exclusion). We therefore:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item report $\IV$ estimates alongside reduced form and first stage;
\item emphasize weak-$\IV$ robust inference (Anderson--Rubin tests, weak-instrument-robust CIs) when the first stage is modest;
\item use falsification outcomes likely affected by politics but not by $\EFW$ components to probe exclusion.
\end{itemize}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section*{PART 2 (REQUIRED) --- EFW $\rightarrow$ Macro}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section*{5) Part 2 Identification}

\subsection*{5.1 Baseline (required): reduced-form dynamic effects via local projections}
For each horizon $h\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, define $Y_{\cid \eid}(h)=y_{\cid,t+h}-y_{\cid,t-1}$ and estimate the horizon-specific reduced-form RD-LP:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\cid \eid}(h) = \alpha_h + \tau^{y}(h)\, D_{\cid \eid} + g_{h}^{-}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}<0] + g_{h}^{+}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}\ge 0] + \bX_{\cid,\,t-1}'\gamma_h + \eta_{\cid \eid h}.
\label{eq:lp_rf}
\end{equation}
This is a \textbf{local projection} because each horizon $h$ is estimated in a separate regression, yielding an impulse-response-style sequence $\{\tau^{y}(h)\}_h$ \citep{Jorda2005}. We interpret $\tau^{y}(h)$ causally under RD continuity, without attributing the effect solely to $\EFW$.

\subsection*{5.2 First-stage dynamics (required): close win $\rightarrow \Delta \EFW$ via local projections}
Analogously, estimate for each horizon:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(h) = \alpha^{\EFW}_h + \pi(h)\, D_{\cid \eid} + g_{h}^{\EFW,-}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}<0] + g_{h}^{\EFW,+}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}\ge 0] + \bX_{\cid,\,t-1}'\kappa_h + \nu_{\cid \eid h}.
\label{eq:lp_fs}
\end{equation}
This is the mechanism/``first stage'' module. It provides evidence about whether close elections generate meaningful institutional movements, and at what horizons and in which $\EFW$ sub-areas.

\subsection*{5.3 OPTIONAL: IV local projections (institutions $\rightarrow$ macro under extra assumptions)}
If (and only if) (\ref{eq:lp_fs}) shows a nontrivial first stage, we optionally estimate an $\IV$-LP:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\cid \eid}(h) = \alpha_h + \beta(h)\, \widehat{\Delta \EFW}_{\cid \eid}(0) + g_{h}^{-}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}<0] + g_{h}^{+}(m_{\cid \eid})\cdot \1[m_{\cid \eid}\ge 0] + \bX_{\cid,\,t-1}'\gamma_h + \tilde{\eta}_{\cid \eid h},
\label{eq:lp_iv}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(0)\equiv \EFW_{\cid,t}-\EFW_{\cid,t-1}$ is the one-year institutional shock at election time, instrumented by $D_{\cid \eid}$ within the close-election window.

\textbf{Interpretation discipline.} $\beta(h)$ is interpreted as the dynamic response of $y$ to a one-unit $\EFW$ shock \emph{only} under:
(i) exclusion (close-win affects $y$ only through $\Delta \EFW$),
(ii) monotonicity (more-market victory weakly increases $\EFW$ for compliers),
and (iii) stable unit treatment value. These may fail; hence this module is optional and presented as such.

\textbf{Weak-IV robust inference.} For (\ref{eq:lp_iv}) we pre-specify reporting of: first-stage $F$-statistics (cluster-robust), Anderson--Rubin confidence sets for $\beta(h)$, and weak-IV-robust tests when relevant.

\subsection*{5.4 Horizon set, outcome families, and multiple testing}
We pre-specify:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item horizons $h\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ (years after election year $t$);
\item outcome families (primary: GDP per capita growth; secondary: inflation; tertiary: investment/trade; tail: crisis indicators);
\item family-wise error rate control within each family across horizons via Holm adjustment (reported alongside unadjusted).
\end{itemize}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section*{6) REQUIRED MODULE A --- Asymmetry of EFW effects}

This module is implemented \textbf{within the LP framework} as the default.

\subsection*{6.1 Asymmetry in institutional shocks (reforms vs reversals)}
Define the election-time $\EFW$ shock and its sign decomposition:
\[
\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(0) \equiv \EFW_{\cid,t}-\EFW_{\cid,t-1},\quad 
\Delta \EFW^{+}_{\cid \eid}\equiv \max(\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(0),0),\quad
\Delta \EFW^{-}_{\cid \eid}\equiv \min(\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(0),0).
\]
We estimate asymmetric LP responses in two complementary ways:

\textbf{(A) Descriptive (non-IV) LP with country controls.}
\begin{equation}
Y_{\cid \eid}(h) = \alpha_h + \beta^{+}(h)\Delta \EFW^{+}_{\cid \eid} + \beta^{-}(h)\Delta \EFW^{-}_{\cid \eid} + \bX_{\cid,\,t-1}'\gamma_h + \eta_{\cid \eid h}.
\label{eq:lp_asym_desc}
\end{equation}
This is not causal; it characterizes whether reforms and reversals exhibit different dynamic correlations.

\textbf{(B) Optional IV-LP asymmetry.} Instrument $\Delta \EFW^{+}_{\cid \eid}$ and $\Delta \EFW^{-}_{\cid \eid}$ using $D_{\cid \eid}$ interacted with predicted sign of the first-stage within the close-election window (implemented via a two-equation system with weak-IV robust inference). Because sign prediction can be fragile, this is explicitly secondary.

\subsection*{6.2 Pre-specified test}
Primary asymmetry test is $H_0:\beta^{+}(h)=\beta^{-}(h)$ for each $h$, with Holm adjustment across horizons.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section*{7) REQUIRED MODULE B --- State dependence of EFW effects}

\subsection*{7.1 Baseline state variables}
We define baseline states $\state_{\cid,t-1}$ using predetermined measures:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item baseline institutional level $\EFW_{\cid,t-1}$ (high vs low freedom),
\item inflation regime (high vs low inflation, based on WDI inflation in $t-1$),
\item political constraints/checks (\texttt{checks} from DPI in $t-1$),
\item crisis state (Laeven--Valencia crisis in $t-1$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{7.2 State-dependent local projections}
For any binary state indicator $S_{\cid,t-1}\in\{0,1\}$, estimate:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\cid \eid}(h) = \alpha_h + \tau_0(h)D_{\cid \eid} + \tau_1(h)\big(D_{\cid \eid}\times S_{\cid,t-1}\big)
+ g_{h}^{-}(m_{\cid \eid})\1[m<0] + g_{h}^{+}(m_{\cid \eid})\1[m\ge 0] + \bX_{\cid,\,t-1}'\gamma_h + \eta_{\cid \eid h}.
\label{eq:lp_state}
\end{equation}
$\tau_0(h)$ is the reduced-form effect in the baseline state ($S=0$) and $\tau_0(h)+\tau_1(h)$ in state $S=1$. We analogously estimate state dependence for the first stage (\ref{eq:lp_fs}).

Optional $\IV$-LP state dependence replaces $D_{\cid \eid}$ with instrumented $\Delta \EFW_{\cid \eid}(0)$ and uses weak-IV robust inference.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section*{8) REQUIRED MODULE C --- Nonlinearities of EFW effects}

\subsection*{8.1 Threshold and spline implementations (LP-default)}
We implement nonlinearities in two pre-specified forms.

\textbf{(A) Threshold at baseline $\EFW$.} Let $H_{\cid,t-1}=\1[\EFW_{\cid,t-1}\ge \mathrm{median}]$. Estimate (\ref{eq:lp_state}) with $S\equiv H$ and interpret as a threshold model.

\textbf{(B) Spline in baseline $\EFW$.} Let $s(\EFW_{\cid,t-1})$ be a restricted cubic spline with 3 knots at pre-specified quantiles (25/50/75). Estimate:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\cid \eid}(h)=\alpha_h+\tau(h)D_{\cid \eid}+\big(D_{\cid \eid}\times s(\EFW_{\cid,t-1})\big)'\theta_h + \text{RD terms} + \bX_{\cid,\,t-1}'\gamma_h+\eta_{\cid \eid h}.
\label{eq:lp_spline}
\end{equation}
We report marginal effects evaluated at selected $\EFW_{\cid,t-1}$ percentiles.

\subsection*{8.2 Nonlinearity tests and multiple testing}
For each horizon, test the joint significance of interaction terms (threshold: $\tau_1(h)=0$; spline: $\theta_h=\mathbf{0}$), with Holm correction across horizons.

\section*{9) Novelty}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Data integration novelty:} a master election--party--government dataset that \emph{uses every dataset in the archive} and makes cross-dataset consistency auditable (merge rates, duplicates, invariants, and quality flags).
\item \textbf{Identification novelty:} a clean separation of RD reduced form, RD ``first stage'' on $\EFW$, and an explicitly optional $\IV$ interpretation---aligned with skeptical referee priors about exclusion in political instruments.
\item \textbf{Method novelty:} LP as the default dynamic estimator within RD windows, enabling transparent impulse-response style estimates plus pre-specified asymmetry/state-dependence/nonlinearity tests.
\end{itemize}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section*{10) Threats / Failure Modes}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Party-ideology misclassification near the cutoff.} Mis-mapping parties to market orientation can bias $m_{\cid \eid}$. Mitigation: ideology cross-validation across V-Party and ParlGov; audit flags for low-confidence party links; robustness to alternative ideology sources (DPI coarse rlc).
\item \textbf{Coalition/parliamentary ambiguity.} ``Winner'' may not form government. Mitigation: validate PM party via ParlGov cabinets; baseline focuses on cases where government formation aligns; retain others as robustness with explicit flag.
\item \textbf{Manipulation of close elections.} Mitigation: McCrary density tests on $m_{\cid \eid}$; heaping tests; placebo on predetermined covariates; restriction to competitive elections using NELDA and NED flags.
\item \textbf{Exclusion failure for $\IV$ layer.} Close elections likely change many policies besides $\EFW$. Mitigation: keep $\IV$ optional; emphasize reduced form; probe channels using $\EFW$ sub-areas and non-$\EFW$ placebo outcomes; use weak-IV robust inference.
\item \textbf{Macro data revisions and comparability.} Mitigation: primary outcomes from WDI, robustness with PWT (explicit vintages) and crisis databases; document vintage used; compare results across sources.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Appendix C: Detailed falsification and robustness menu (implementable checklist)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Placebo cutoffs: shift cutoff to $\pm 2$pp and re-estimate.
\item Placebo outcomes: pre-election outcomes ($h<0$), and outcomes plausibly unrelated to market orientation but sensitive to politics (flagged ex ante).
\item Alternative margin definitions: NED seat-share margin vs CLEA aggregated vote-share margin (parliamentary); first-round vs second-round margins when applicable (presidential).
\item Alternative ideology sources: V-Party vs ParlGov state\_market; DPI coarse rlc categories.
\item Leave-one-region-out and leave-one-country-out sensitivity (pooled estimates).
\end{enumerate}
